## The Problem

Practice matters in business education, but opportunities to practice are uneven. A mock interview needs someone to ask questions. A case-study presentation needs someone to listen and give feedback. For teachers working with an entire classroom, that individual attention is difficult to provide repeatedly.

Nathan and I developed CAISE around that gap. Our backgrounds in DECA and FBLA gave us a connection to students preparing for business competitions. The aim was to make independent practice more accessible while supporting the educators working with those students.

In the announcement, we introduced the product ahead of a planned closed beta. The problem extended beyond competition preparation to mock interviews and keeping students engaged with business education.

## Generating a Practice Case

### Starting with the skills to practice

Students choose an industry, a focus, and key terms or performance indicators. Those inputs give the generated scenario a learning objective: practice a particular skill rather than respond to an arbitrary business question.

![CAISE case generation interface](https://www.caise.app/generateImage.png "Fig. 1. Configuring a practice case")

### Turning preparation into a repeatable process

The product connects case generation to presentation and evaluation in one sequence. The student can move from understanding a scenario to articulating a response and reviewing feedback.

## Presenting a Solution

### Practicing the explanation

Students present their solution to an AI judge using a structured presentation flow. This adds a different kind of practice from reading a case and thinking through an answer: the student has to explain the recommendation.

![CAISE presentation interface](https://www.caise.app/presentImage.png "Fig. 2. Presenting to an AI judge")

## Feedback and Another Attempt

### Making the score useful

The evaluation screen pairs scores with explanations and detailed feedback. That makes the assessment a starting point for the next practice session.

![CAISE evaluation interface](https://www.caise.app/evaluateImage.png "Fig. 3. Scoring and presentation feedback")

### Supporting the classroom

The broader product direction included custom simulations assigned by teachers and a way to follow student improvement. Independent practice and classroom use address the same constraint from different sides: giving students more chances to rehearse without requiring a teacher to personally judge every attempt.
